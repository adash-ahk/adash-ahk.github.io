## **Celebrate Uniqueness with `.uniq`**

`.uniq` is your go-to method for eliminating duplicates and keeping your data clean and focused. Whether you're organizing a list of items, deduplicating entries from multiple sources, or simply ensuring every element is unique, `.uniq` gives you a streamlined array with just the essentials. Immutability is at the heart of `.uniq`, so each time you call it, your original array remains untouched, giving you a fresh, clutter-free version without impacting your initial data.

### Example 1: Cleaning Up User Tags

When handling user data, like tags or categories, duplicates can sometimes creep in. With `.uniq`, you can remove repeated entries, creating a list of only unique tags.

```autohotkey
userTags := ["Admin", "Moderator", "Admin", "User", "Guest", "User"]
uniqueTags := _.uniq(userTags)
; => ["Admin", "Moderator", "User", "Guest"]
```

With the duplicates gone, you're left with a concise, organized list of tags, ready for display or further use.

### Example 2: Filtering Unique Objects
Suppose you have a list of users, where some entries are identical duplicates. By using `.uniq`, you can keep the first occurrence of each unique user.

```autohotkey
users := [{name: "Arthur", age: 35, city: "New York"}
	, {name: "Arthur ", age: 35, city: "New York"}
	, {name: "Dominick", age: 37, city: "Los Angeles"}
	, {name: "Eames", age: 40, city: "Mombasa"}
	, {name: "Dominick", age: 37, city: "Los Angeles"}]

uniqueUsers := _.uniq(users)
; => [{name: "Arthur", age: 35, city: "New York"}
;	, {name: "Dominick", age: 37, city: "Los Angeles"}
;	, {name: "Eames", age: 40, city: "Mombasa"}]
```

This uniqueUsers array now contains only unique entries based on the combined properties of each user object, reducing any identical duplicates. I have found this especially useful when creating objects from online data, where users or entries may be listed multiple times on a page or API.

It’s all about showcasing only what’s unique. Whether managing tags, event lists, or any collection prone to duplicates, `.uniq` helps keep your data fresh, clean, and distinct. What else could you accomplish with `.uniq`?
